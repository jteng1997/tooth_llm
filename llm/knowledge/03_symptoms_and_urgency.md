## Why the type of pain matters

Dentists pay close attention to how pain behaves, because the pattern
says a lot about what is going on inside the tooth.

Pain that flares with something cold or sweet and then fades within a few
seconds usually points to irritation that has not yet damaged the nerve
permanently. This is uncomfortable but often treatable simply.

Pain that keeps aching well after the cold has gone, pain that starts on
its own without a trigger, or pain that wakes someone at night, suggests
the nerve inside the tooth is more seriously affected. This kind of pain
tends not to settle by itself and is usually seen sooner.

Pain when biting down on a particular tooth can mean the problem has
reached the tissues around the root tip.

## Warning signs that need same-day care

Swelling of the face or gums, fever alongside dental pain, or any
difficulty swallowing or breathing can mean an infection is spreading
beyond the tooth. These need urgent care the same day, from a dentist or,
if breathing or swallowing is affected, from emergency medical services.

A tooth that has been knocked out, loosened or broken by an injury is
also time-critical, because what can be saved depends heavily on how
quickly it is treated.

## Why sensitivity can happen with no cavity visible

Short-lived sensitivity does not always mean decay. Gums receding and
exposing the root, a worn or cracked surface, grinding, a recent filling,
or decay hidden between teeth where a photograph cannot see, can all
cause it. This is one reason a screening photo cannot rule anything out.

## How to explain each urgency level

For the emergency level, be clear and calm: the symptoms described can be
signs of a spreading infection or a time-critical injury, and care is
needed today.

For the urgent level: the pattern of pain suggests the inside of a tooth
may be involved, and this is usually seen within a few days rather than
waiting.

For the soon level: something was spotted, or symptoms were reported, and
it is worth an appointment in the coming weeks. Treating early is simpler.

For the routine level: nothing was visible in these photos and no
concerning symptoms were described, so continuing with regular checkups
is reasonable. This is not the same as saying nothing is wrong.
