## Can I skip the dentist if I brush better or use a special toothpaste?

Good brushing and fluoride can slow down or sometimes stop very early
damage, but once a hole has formed the tooth cannot rebuild it. Better
habits are worth it either way, but they do not replace the visit.

## Are you sure it is a cavity?

No. This looks at a photograph, which cannot show how deep anything goes
or what is between the teeth. It can flag something that turns out to be
a stain or a filling, and it can miss real decay. Only a dentist
examining the tooth can confirm it.

## You found nothing, so my teeth are fine, right?

Not quite. It means nothing was visible on the biting surfaces in these
photos. Decay between teeth, under the gums, or inside a tooth would not
show up here. Regular checkups are still the way to catch those.

## Can I just take painkillers until it goes away?

Painkillers can help with discomfort for a short while, but they do not
treat the cause, and dental pain that keeps returning usually means
something is progressing. If pain lingers, wakes you at night, or comes
with swelling, that needs to be seen rather than managed at home.

## Can I drill it, dig it out, or fix it myself?

No. Trying to remove decay or treat a tooth at home risks serious damage
and infection. This always needs a dentist.

## Should I put aspirin or anything else directly on the tooth or gum?

No. Putting painkillers directly against the gum can burn the tissue. Any
painkiller should be taken as directed on the packet, not placed on the
tooth.

## Which specific antibiotic should I take?

That is not something this tool or any assistant can advise. Antibiotics
for dental infection need a prescriber who has examined you. If you have
swelling or fever, that is a reason to be seen today.

## How much will it cost, and how long will it take?

That depends on what the dentist finds and where you are, so this tool
cannot say. A clinic can usually give an estimate once they have looked.

## Can I wait a few months, I am busy?

That is your decision to make, and only a dentist can tell you what the
real risk of waiting is in your case. What is generally true is that
decay does not improve on its own, and treating it later usually means
more extensive work.

## Is this an official diagnosis I can show someone?

No. This is a screening aid. It has no clinical standing. You are welcome
to show the summary to a dentist as a starting point for a conversation.
