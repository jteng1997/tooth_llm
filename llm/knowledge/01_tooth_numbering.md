## What the two-digit tooth numbers mean

Dentists label each tooth with two digits. The first digit says which
quarter of the mouth the tooth is in, and the second says how far back it
sits, counting from the front middle.

The four quarters, seen from the patient's own point of view, are: 1 for
the upper right, 2 for the upper left, 3 for the lower left, and 4 for
the lower right.

The second digit runs from 1 at the front middle to 8 at the very back.
So tooth 16 is the upper right first molar, and tooth 36 is the lower
left first molar.

## Plain-word names for each position

Position 1 and 2 are the incisors, the flat front teeth used for biting.
Position 3 is the canine, the pointed corner tooth. Positions 4 and 5 are
the premolars. Positions 6, 7 and 8 are the molars, the big flat-topped
teeth at the back used for grinding. Position 8 is the wisdom tooth,
which many adults never grow or have removed.

## How to describe a tooth to a patient

Always pair the number with an everyday description. For example, tooth
16 can be described as the upper right back molar, and tooth 24 as the
upper left premolar, the smaller tooth behind the pointed one.

## Children's teeth

Baby teeth use a different set of quarter digits, 5 through 8, and only
positions 1 to 5. A child in mixed dentition has both baby and adult
teeth at once, so a gap or a missing tooth is often completely normal at
that age.

## What an occlusal photo shows

An occlusal photo looks straight down, or straight up, at the biting
surfaces of one arch. It shows the chewing surfaces of the back teeth
and the biting edges of the front teeth.

These photos are usually taken with a small mirror placed inside the
mouth, which means the image is flipped left to right compared with
looking directly at the person.
