# Form B — blind vignette labelling

## What we are asking

You read a short patient description and say **how soon that person should be
seen**. That's all. No diagnosis, no treatment plan.

You will not see what our system said, and you will not see our own answer
key, because your labels are the yardstick we measure the system against. If
you see our answers first, the measurement is worthless.

## The four levels

Use these definitions, which match SDCEP's timescales:

| Level | Means |
|---|---|
| **EMERGENCY** | Care today. Either a medical emergency (hospital / emergency department now) or a dental emergency (contact a dentist within the hour). |
| **URGENT** | Seen by a dentist within 24 hours. |
| **SOON** | Seen by a dentist within 7 days. |
| **ROUTINE** | Nothing sooner than the next routine check-up. |

Two extra boxes on every sheet:

- **Not enough information** — tick when you could not decide from what is
  written. This is a useful answer, not a failure; it tells us the interview
  is not asking something it should.
- **Route (EMERGENCY only)** — hospital or dentist. We record it for research;
  the patient is shown a single emergency screen either way.

And one free-text box: **what drove your decision** (a few words is plenty).
This is how we learn whether we are reasoning the same way when we agree.

## What each vignette contains

- The patient's own words, as they would have typed them.
- One line about the photo, in plain English, e.g. "the photo flagged possible
  decay on the upper right first molar", or "the photos showed nothing", or
  "the photos were too blurry to read".

Assume the photo line is accurate. We know the detector is unreliable (29%
sensitivity at our threshold); that is measured separately and is not what
this form is about.

## How the sheets work

- Cases arrive in random order, mixed across levels. Roughly 220 in total: 200
  test cases, 60 interview transcripts, and the 20 older rule cases rendered
  readable. A 60-case subset is worth having on its own if time is short.
- About 1 minute each. Please stop when tired rather than push through; speed
  errors are worse for us than a smaller sample.
- If two of you are available, label independently and do not discuss until
  both are finished. We will report where you disagreed, and take the
  consensus after you have talked it through.
- If only one of you is available, we will re-send 20 cases a fortnight later
  to measure how consistent you are with yourself. That is not a test of you;
  it is a number the paper needs.

## What happens to your labels

They become the reference standard. We report, separately:
- how often the system was *less* urgent than you (the number that matters),
- how often it was more urgent,
- agreement overall and by level,
- and how often **our own protocol** disagreed with you, which tells us
  whether the protocol itself is wrong.

We will not change your labels. If we think one is a mistake, we will ask you.

## Sheet format

Sheets are generated by `make_form_b.py` once the vignette text exists, and
are written outside the repository (`runs/dentist/`), because they must not be
committed next to the answers. One row per case:

```
Case 037
Patient: "..."
Photo: ...
Level:  [ ] EMERGENCY   [ ] URGENT   [ ] SOON   [ ] ROUTINE   [ ] Not enough information
If EMERGENCY, route: [ ] hospital  [ ] dentist
What drove your decision: ______________________________________________
```
